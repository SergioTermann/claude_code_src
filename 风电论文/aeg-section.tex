% ---------------------------------------------------------------- METHOD
\subsection{Ambiguity-Entropy Gating}
\label{sec:aeg}
Scope binding (the Domain Relationship Model) resolves fault-code ambiguity \emph{after} a scope constraint $\Omega(q,s_t)$ becomes available. In practice the constraint is frequently absent in the first turn, yet always asking a technician for the turbine model is intrusive and slows field work. We therefore introduce an information-theoretic gate that decides, \emph{before} retrieval, whether a fault code is ambiguous enough to justify a disambiguation prompt.

Let $\mathcal{M}(c)$ be the set of distinct fault meanings carried by code $c$ across $\mathcal{F}(c)$, and let $p_c(m)$ denote the empirical probability of meaning $m$. The \emph{ambiguity entropy} of code $c$ is
\begin{equation}
H(c) = -\!\!\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}(c)}\!\! p_c(m)\,\log_2 p_c(m).
\label{eq:aeg_entropy}
\end{equation}
A single-meaning code has $H(c)=0$; a code whose meanings are spread across many turbine models has large $H(c)$. Given a gate threshold $\tau$, the pre-retrieval policy is
\begin{equation}
\pi(c)=
\begin{cases}
\text{retrieve and answer}, & H(c)\le\tau \ \text{or}\ \Omega(q,s_t)\neq\varnothing,\\[2pt]
\text{request turbine model}, & H(c)>\tau \ \text{and}\ \Omega(q,s_t)=\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:aeg_policy}
\end{equation}
The gate turns disambiguation from a passive fallback (``prompt only after retrieval looks ambiguous'') into an active, quantifiable pre-retrieval risk control: only codes whose meaning distribution is genuinely uncertain trigger a prompt, and $\tau$ directly tunes the trade-off between intrusiveness and mis-hit risk. Because the gate only restricts \emph{which} codes require a scope constraint, it never enlarges the candidate set and preserves the admissibility property $\mathcal{A}(q,s_t)\subseteq\mathcal{F}(c)$ established for scope binding.

% ------------------------------------------------------------ EXPERIMENT
\subsection{Ambiguity-Entropy Gating: Effectiveness}
\label{sec:aeg_eval}
We evaluate the gate on all $11{,}476$ valid fault records ($4{,}463$ unique codes) under a \emph{leave-one-out} (LOO) protocol that measures true generalization rather than a closed-form expectation. Each record is treated as a first-turn query that reports a code without model context; a majority-vote retrieval baseline returns the most frequent meaning among the \emph{remaining} same-code records. If no other record shares the code, the system \emph{abstains} (``insufficient evidence''), which is not counted as a mis-hit because the system declines rather than answers wrongly. Without model context, $76.2\%$ of queries are answerable and $23.8\%$ abstain; the answerable-set mis-hit rate is $51.7\%$. Restricting to the queried turbine model lowers the mis-hit rate to $35.6\%$ but raises abstention to $83.8\%$, exposing genuine data sparsity at the $\mathrm{code}\times\mathrm{model}$ granularity. (A closed-form expected-value analysis, reported in the supplementary script, gives an optimistic $28.8\%$ baseline; the LOO figures below are more conservative and more faithful.)

\noindent\textbf{Entropy predicts mis-hit.}
$H(c)$ and the LOO mis-hit rate are strongly correlated (Pearson $0.86$, Spearman $0.92$). Mean mis-hit rises monotonically with entropy (Table~\ref{tab:aeg_buckets}), from $0\%$ for single-meaning codes to $90.6\%$ for $H>2$. As $H$ and mis-hit both derive from $p_c$, this correlation is partly intrinsic and establishes $H$ as a gate-able proxy; the independent decision value is shown below.

\noindent\textbf{Entropy gating is Pareto-optimal.}
Under an equal ask-budget (fraction of queries prompted), ranking codes by $H$ leaves a lower residual mis-hit than random gating and dominates frequency ranking (Table~\ref{tab:aeg_tradeoff}). At a $30\%$ budget the residual mis-hit drops to $24.4\%$ versus $36.2\%$ for random gating---a $32.5\%$ relative reduction---confirming that entropy is a valuable ranking signal, not merely that asking helps.

\noindent\textbf{Ask-gain is entropy-driven (independent check).}
The mis-hit actually removed by asking for the model, $\mathrm{gain}(c)$, is determined by the $\mathrm{code}\times\mathrm{model}$ joint distribution and is therefore not a function of $H(c)$. It nonetheless correlates with $H$ (Pearson $0.70$, Spearman $0.72$): asking is worthless for single-meaning codes ($0\%$ gain) and removes $84.5\%$ of mis-hits for $H>2$ codes, with high-entropy codes ($H>2$) yielding about $3\times$ the gain of low-entropy ambiguous ones ($0<H\le1$). This independent evidence justifies deciding \emph{which} codes to disambiguate by entropy. At the operating point that gates all ambiguous codes, prompting on $62.2\%$ of queries reduces the LOO mis-hit rate from $51.7\%$ to $\approx 0$.
